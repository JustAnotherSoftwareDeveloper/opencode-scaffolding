# Task-Sensitive Source Quality

Assess a source in relation to the question, the specific claim, and the stated intended use. Choose only the dimensions that could materially affect the answer, and explain why each selected dimension matters. This is qualitative appraisal, not a universal source grade.

Depending on the task, consider:

- **Fit and applicability:** Does the source address the relevant question, population, place, period, or context?
- **Evidence type and method:** Is the material suitable for the claim, and are the method and limitations visible?
- **Authority and perspective:** What expertise or authority matters for this information need? Who created the source, for what purpose, and whose perspective may be missing?
- **Mediation and omissions:** Is the evidence direct or summarized, and what transformation, selection, silence, or qualification may affect it?
- **Currency:** How quickly does this subject change, and could newer evidence materially affect the claim? A publication date is not proof of current page content.
- **Corroboration and disagreement:** Do independent lines of evidence support the claim, share a common origin, or conflict? Explain relationships rather than counting sources.
- **Consequences and traceability:** What could follow from an error, and can the claim be traced to an inspected supporting passage?

Do not apply every dimension mechanically. A primary source may be direct evidence for one question and insufficient or misleading for another; secondary synthesis may be necessary context. Prestige, recency, source count, and document type do not independently establish quality.

Higher consequences call for closer scrutiny of critical claims and more visible uncertainty, not an automatic source-type preference or invented confidence threshold. Do not use a universal hierarchy, numeric score, fixed weighting formula, or cross-domain validity claim. The available evidence supports contextual appraisal dimensions; it does not establish universally valid weights or a general-purpose ranking method.
