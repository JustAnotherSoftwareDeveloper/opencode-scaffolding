# Evidence Synthesis

## Build A Claim Record

Identify the material claims needed to answer the normalized question. Give each a stable claim ID and link it to source IDs and inspected passages or page locations. Record evidence against the claim it supports; do not cite a search excerpt as if the underlying page was inspected.

For each material claim, distinguish:

- what an inspected source explicitly reports;
- what the researcher infers by comparing or interpreting sources;
- evidence that qualifies or contradicts the claim; and
- what remains unknown, unverified, or unsupported.

Use [source quality](source-quality.md) to explain why each source and evidence type fits—or does not fit—the particular claim and intended use. Source quantity, prestige, recency, or primary-source status alone does not establish support.

## Write The Answer

Answer the question directly at the requested depth. Organize findings around the question rather than the order in which pages appeared. Preserve relevant disagreement, scope limits, and differences in method or population. Do not resolve conflicting sources by silently selecting the most confident wording or the largest source count.

State an interpretation as an interpretation and make its supporting sources and reasoning traceable. Qualify conclusions to the evidence inspected. When evidence cannot answer a material part of the question, say what is missing and why; do not fill gaps with unsupported certainty.

## Keep The Ownership Boundary

Report descriptive findings and their evidentiary limits. Do not turn the synthesis into an evidence-calibrated assessment, recommendation, option decision, or durable proposal. If the user requests one of those terminal results, leave that work to its owning operation rather than broadening this operation.
